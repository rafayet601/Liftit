# Glute Focused

Research reviewed on October 1, 2026. This is a practical training template for adult lifters with a glute-growth goal, designed with female lifters in mind and available to anyone. No research establishes one universally best glute program. The exact Liftit schedule, exercise substitutions and deload timing are programming choices rather than a replicated study protocol.

## Schedules

The default generator creates an eight-week, five-day block:

| Day | Session | Emphasis |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Monday | Quads | Squat, split squat, leg extension, calves |
| Tuesday | Upper A | Chest, back, shoulders, arms |
| Thursday | Whole Legs | Leg press, hip thrust, leg curl, calves; two sets per exercise |
| Friday | Upper B | Shoulders, back, chest, arms |
| Saturday | Glutes | Hip thrust, Romanian deadlift, leg curl, cable kickback, hip abduction |

Three lower sessions can fit into three to five total training days. Shorter schedules mix upper-body work into lower sessions. The two-lower-session option supports two to four total days and omits Whole Legs. Sample schedules provide at least 48 hours between lower sessions, including across the weekly boundary. Training days are ordered sessions, not enforced calendar appointments.

## Dose and progression

- Full-gym five-day intermediate targets include 17 glute-involving working sets and two abduction sets weekly. The two-lower-day option includes 13 and two respectively. Squats, lunges and hinges also train other muscles; these counts are not equivalent to 17 or 13 isolated glute sets, nor an individually proven optimal dose.
- Beginners use one fewer set per exercise, with a minimum of one, and a base RPE target of 7. Intermediate and advanced lifters start at RPE 8. Advanced status does not automatically inflate volume.
- Weeks 1–2 use slightly easier effort. Subsequent building weeks retain working-set counts and progress repetitions and load. The final week halves set targets with rounding and lowers RPE by two. This goal does not use the strength-oriented realization phase.
- Main lifts use 8–12 repetitions, supporting leg work 10–15, and kickbacks/abduction 12–20. Rest is 150 seconds for main lifts, 120 for split squats and Whole Legs compounds, and 75–90 for other accessories. Longer rest is appropriate when needed to maintain performance.
- Warm-up sets precede the listed working sets. Unilateral prescriptions are repetitions per side. Keep main exercises consistent during a block. The existing progression engine uses logged repetitions and effort to suggest holding or increasing load; there are no fixed starting weights based on gender.
- Adjust sets, load or schedule when recovery or technique worsens. Sleep, energy and menstrual symptoms can inform individual adjustments. Menstrual-cycle phase does not automatically alter the program.

The setup offers six-, eight- and twelve-week blocks. Onboarding creates an eight-week block. Exercise cues survive local saving, sharing and starting a workout. Swapping exercises clears obsolete cues.

## Exercise substitutions

The full gym uses cable kickbacks and a hip-abduction machine. Dumbbell and minimal presets substitute quadruped hip extensions and side-lying abduction. Home pressing/pulling uses dumbbell presses, rows and pullovers. The minimal preset requires a kettlebell and a secure pull-up bar. Bodyweight alternatives may require added resistance or an exercise change once they become too easy. These substitutions are practical options, not proven equivalent hypertrophy interventions.

Abduction is an accessory for hip abductors and should not be presented as the primary evidence for gluteus-maximus growth. The thrust, squat/lunge and hinge combination supplies the main hip-extension work. Cable kickbacks are included as requested, with controlled technique and recoverable accessory volume; comparative long-term evidence does not establish them as superior to the main lifts.

## Research and decisions

1. [ACSM 2026 resistance-training guidance](https://acsm.org/resistance-training-guidelines-update-2026/): supports progressive, individualized training, meaningful weekly volume and consistency. Complex periodization and training every set to failure are not prerequisites for most healthy adults. Used to choose repeatable exercises, effort targets and adaptable volume.
2. [Krause Neto et al., 2025 glute hypertrophy systematic review](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/physiology/articles/10.3389/fphys.2025.1542334/full): synthesizes twelve articles and supports several resisted hip-extension exercises, alone or combined. Its mixed populations, protocols and study quality do not identify a universally optimal split. Used to support movement variety rather than an EMG-only exercise ranking.
3. [Plotkin et al., 2023 randomized squat/hip-thrust trial](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/physiology/articles/10.3389/fphys.2023.1279170/full): nine weeks in untrained young adults produced similar glute growth between volume-equated squat and hip-thrust groups; squats produced greater thigh growth. Used to separate quad and glute emphasis while retaining both movements.
4. [Kassiano et al., 2024 trial in untrained young women](https://doi.org/10.47206/ijsc.v4i1.284): adding hip thrusts to leg press and stiff-leg deadlift increased glute muscle thickness more over ten weeks. The added exercise also increased training volume. Used to support including hip thrusts, not to claim a volume-independent advantage. The trial trained three days weekly, so it does not directly validate Liftit’s two-lower-day option.
5. [Schoenfeld et al., 2019 frequency meta-analysis](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30558493/): volume-equated comparisons did not show a meaningful hypertrophy advantage for higher frequency. Used to offer two or three lower sessions based on preference, manageable work and recovery.
6. [Refalo et al., 2025 sex-differences meta-analysis](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40028215/): supports similar relative hypertrophy potential in women and men. Used to avoid arbitrary lighter loads or gender restrictions.
7. [D’Souza et al., 2026 randomized trial](https://eprints.ncl.ac.uk/312484): twenty-four healthy eumenorrheic women completed a hormone-verified, unilateral trial over approximately twelve weeks. Menstrual-phase-based training did not outperform continuous training for the measured muscle and strength outcomes. This is not a claim that symptoms never affect individual performance or that its findings cover every hormonal or clinical context.

## Verification scope

Automated tests cover both lower-session options, all offered day counts and equipment presets, exercise resolution, push/pull balance, beginner dosing, goal-specific week scaling, persistence/sharing, onboarding, program creation and session-launch cues. These checks validate software behavior; they do not establish individual training outcomes.
